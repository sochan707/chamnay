# Chamnay Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

University students who manage their own daily income, expenses, bills, and savings and want a clear view of what remains available.

## Product Purpose

Chamnay is a phone-friendly budget planner that makes manual money tracking calm and understandable. Success means a student can record money in and out, recognize where spending went, correct mistakes, and see the effect on their available balance without needing financial expertise.

## Positioning

Chamnay presents budgeting as a small, approachable daily habit rather than a dense accounting workflow. Its first prototype favors clear manual control and a reassuring overview over bank connections or automated financial advice.

## Operating Context

The current prototype is evaluated in desktop and mobile browsers. It begins with illustrative financial figures, stores user changes in the current browser, and is demonstrated through the Home and Settings flows.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The Free product plan covers Home, Bills, Savings, and Settings.
- The implemented Home flow supports adding, editing, deleting, filtering, and summarizing income and expenses.
- Expense categories include protected ready-made choices and locally persisted personal categories with a name, icon, and color.
- Deleting a personal category requires its expenses to be reassigned first.
- Bills, savings goals, reminders, and dark mode remain planned prototype work.
- Browser-local storage is sufficient for this prototype; accounts, bank connections, automatic imports, cross-device sync, financial advice, and production subscriptions are out of scope.

## Brand Commitments

The product name is Chamnay. Existing copy uses a calm, supportive voice, including “A little space for your money” and “Small steps count.” The incumbent logo, icon assets, and interface identity are established in the current application and should be preserved by feature extensions.

## Evidence on Hand

The repository contains a working frontend prototype, sample transaction data, project planning documents, and responsive UI assets. The project has not run a demand survey, so claims about the size or frequency of student budgeting problems remain assumptions.

## Product Principles

- Keep routine entry and correction quick.
- Make balances, categories, and consequences easy to recognize.
- Let users control their own data and classifications.
- Treat private financial information cautiously and collect only what the prototype needs.
- Label sample data and unproven business assumptions honestly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Core money and category tasks must work with keyboard navigation, visible focus, accessible dialogs and validation, readable contrast, and responsive layouts at mobile, tablet, and desktop sizes.
