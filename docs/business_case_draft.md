# Business Case: Budget Planner

**Status:** Milestone 1 draft for group review — 20 October 2026  
**Team:** 4 people  
**Decision asked for:** Agree on the project idea, its first scope, and a plan the group can afford.  
**Evidence:** The group has decided not to run a survey. Claims about what students need are still assumptions.

## 1. ROI

**ROI means return on investment:** what the project gives back for the money and time spent on it.

The hoped-for return is a tool that helps students see where their money goes and what they have left to spend. A small first version would let the group test whether the idea is useful before spending more time or money.

We **cannot give a money ROI yet**. The group has not chosen a price, a way to earn money, or a target number of users. These are `TBD`. We should not claim savings or revenue that we have not measured.

## 2. Problem

Some university students may find it hard to track small costs, plan for bills, and know what they can still spend. They may use notes, paper, a spreadsheet, or another budget app. We have not measured how many students have this problem or how much it affects them.

**Main users:** University students who manage their own daily costs. Workers may be a later group, but the first plan is for students.

## 3. Solution

Budget Planner is a simple, phone-friendly app. The first version would let a user:

- Add money coming in and going out by hand.
- Set a monthly limit for each type of spending, such as food or travel.
- See spending against each limit and an estimate of what is left.
- See a short monthly summary.

The first version would leave out bank links, automatic imports, money advice, and social features. The group can change this list if it agrees on a better scope.

## 4. Risk

| Risk | What we would do |
| --- | --- |
| Students may not find the app useful. | Keep the first version small and check how people use it in a pilot. |
| Users may not want to enter money details. | Ask for only the details needed and explain how they are kept. |
| The work may take more time or money than planned. | Agree on the must-have parts first and cut less important parts if needed. |
| We have no survey data about demand. | Mark user needs as assumptions and do not claim that demand is proven. |
